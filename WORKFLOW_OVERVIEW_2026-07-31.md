Background:

Lost Point Demolition is a demolition service provider located in Utah that serves Salt Lake County, Utah County, and Summit County. It is headquartered in Orem, Utah, and has been in business for eight years.

* Nick:  Crew 1 Foreman  
* Alex:  Crew 2 Foreman  
* Brady:  Crew 3 Foreman  
* Cade:  Crew 4 Foreman  
* Matt:  CFO  
* Other hourly employees

Dane and Jackson are salaried.  I (Matt) are paid contractually. All of the foremen and other hourly employees are paid hourly.

Problem

Many tasks, in particular admin-related tasks, are manual and need to be automated so that information and data are more readily available to make better business decisions.

In general, here is the current workflow:

* Bid Request  
* Estimate Sent  
* Estimate Accepted/Denied  
* Job Scheduled  
* Job In Progress  
* Job Completed  
* Invoice Sent  
* Invoice Paid

Bid Request

We received work from two different customers. One is a property owner (typically residential), who is typically a homeowner looking for some level of demolition completed in their home. Most of these are either referrals or come through as bid requests on our website. Most of our business comes through working relationships that we have with contractors who are running some kind of a remodel job and are subcontracting the demolition portion out to us.

During the bid request stage, we are gathering information around the scope of the project, using our internal bidding calculator to determine how much time we should allocate and to provide an estimate of our labor costs, direct costs, as well as an overhead allocation.

Estimation

At this stage, Dane and Jackson calculate an estimate of the price of the job.  That depends on the scope and how much time we believe it will take to complete that job with the number of employees that are assigned to that job. They then use an internal bidding calculator to estimate our labor expense based on the number of employees and the number of productive hours. We then assign an overhead allocation as a cost, assign any other direct costs (materials, dump loads, rentals, etc.), then add in a profit margin and credit card fee, etc.

An estimate is created through GoHighLevel's estimation system, where we create or input line items and a description of the work that's being done with the associated costs

Estimate accepted/denied

Once an estimate has been sent, a customer can either accept it through GoHiLevel's estimation tool or, in our communications with them, they just ask when we can get started.  Or the estimate is denied, in which case the job is lost.

We have no data on how fast we get them an estimate, or how many bids we are receiving versus how many we are winning or denying, and the reason that those bids were denied and all that. It seems that the faster that we get a client estimate, the more likely that we will get awarded the job, but we don't have any data to support that. We don't have any data supporting the types of jobs based on the scope of work that we are receiving.

Job scheduled

When a job is scheduled, information about that job goes into a Google Calendar. We have four different crews, and the night before the job starts, a message is sent in Slack to that particular crew's Slack channel with the details about the job:

* the address  
* the contractor or client name  
* contact information  
* Scope of the job  
* any other pertinent information that is related to that job

When a job is scheduled, many times it isn't necessarily assigned to a crew at that point, but it will be assigned to a crew no later than 4 p.m. of the day before the Slack message goes out when a job is started.

Job Start

When a job is started, the foreman (the employee who oversees the job site) completes a job checklist. It's a form to fill out with some fields that he has to complete. There are three versions of the job checklist:

* Job Started  
* Job in Progress  
* Job Completed  
* 

The foreman will also take before pictures of the job and submit them to his own Slack channel.

Currently, we don't have a reliable way to be able to assign labor hours to a specific job. We have been creating a project in our time tracking tool through Gusto, but we have to manually create that project for someone to assign to clock their hours to a specific job. We then manually have to delete it once the job was completed, and that hasn't been consistent. Our employees are using a time tracking app through Gusto, so the behavior is already there, but we don't have a reliable way to automatically create jobs or job codes and keep track of those.

We also use Divi/Bill for our credit card, as well as their expense management software. There is a way to be able to assign transactions to different budgets or different projects. Again, we don't have a reliable way to automatically create the job name for those dollars to be assigned there for expenses that are related to a specific job, like materials or dump fees, etc.

Job in progress

If it's a multi-day job, at the end of each day he will submit the Job in Progress form. Once the job is completed, he then submits the Job Completed form, which includes some information, including after pictures of the job. On the Job Started form and the Job in Progress form, he also includes pictures of the job, and that information automatically writes to that firm's or that crew's Slack channel.

If there are change orders, the foreman will communicate those change orders to Dane and Jackson, who will estimate the cost of the change order and communicate that either directly to the client or to the foreman to communicate that with the client. We then have to remember to include the change order in the invoice because we don't have a way to track change orders at the moment.

Job completed

Once the job is completed, the foreman completes the job completed form as outlined above.

He also takes pictures of the job completed and submits them to his own Slack channel.

Invoice sent

After the job is completed, Dane or Jackson will review the job, manually generate an invoice with line items and a description for each item, and input the cost of each item.   If there are change orders for that job, that has to be included on the invoice as well.  The invoice will be sent to the customer through GHL's payment system, which is routed through Stripe.

Invoice paid

Once a client or customer pays, then we are completely done with this job.

We also like to ask for a Google review, but we don't automatically do that. It's also a manual process.
